The Dilemma

Same destination. Different group chats.

College Station is packed with students heading to the exact same places every weekend — home to Houston or Dallas, airport runs, or simple grocery trips on Texas Ave. Yet discovering each other is chaos.

GroupMe • TAMU Rideshare '26 Friday 1:14 PM

"Anyone driving to Houston this Friday around 4? Will split gas!"

Lost under 84 other unread messages
Snapchat Story • Maya Friday 2:05 PM

"Leaving CS at 5 PM for Katy/Houston. Have 3 empty seats in my Civic."

Expires in 18 hours
Discord • Zachry Engineering Saturday 10:20 AM

"Can someone swing by Park West for Walmart? Uber is charging $22 for 2 miles 💀"

Zero replies
The Inevitable Hallway Moment Sunday Night

"Wait, YOU WERE GOING TO HOUSTON TOO?! Why didn’t you tell me?!"

Thousands of empty seats.

On any given Friday, hundreds of students drive alone on Highway 6, Highway 21, and I-45, while hundreds of other students are frantically checking four different apps hoping someone is heading their way.

Surge pricing hurts students.

Commercial rideshare apps charge $20 to $30 just to go from campus to the grocery store. For cross-city trips to DFW or Houston, commercial buses have inflexible schedules and inconvenient stations.

"What if finding your way there was as easy as finding your people?"

Campus Field Research

What Aggies told us: 23-student campus survey.

Before writing code, we surveyed Texas A&M drivers and passengers across Bryan–College Station about their travel habits, empty seats, and safety requirements. The data validated the exact design of Aggie Ride.

directions_car Driver Findings (52% of respondents)

What would make drivers offer empty seats?

  • 1 "Only on trips I'm already making": Drivers don't want to become commercial chauffeurs. They only want to share existing journeys — directly validating our Ride Intent philosophy.
  • 2 "Choosing the pickup spot myself (5–10 min max)": Drivers strongly reject wandering through complex apartment gates — validating our 7 Predefined Campus Meeting Hubs.
  • 3 Gas money & NetID verification: Offsetting Texas gas costs while ensuring 100% verified TAMU student identity.
hail Passenger Findings (48% of respondents)

Where passengers need to go & what builds trust:

  • 1 Primary destinations: Grocery runs (Walmart / H-E-B: 73%), weekend trips home to Houston/Austin/DFW (45%), and campus events.
  • 2 Top safety factors: Official @tamu.edu directory verification (73%), driver reviews (64%), live trip sharing, and OTP boarding passes.
  • 3 Current friction: Relying on walking in Texas heat, sporadic bus routes, or pleading in disconnected GroupMe chats.
“Splitting gas money, only verified TAMU students, and only on trips I'm already making.” — Surveyed Texas A&M Student Driver (About once a week off-campus, 3+ seats open)
“TAMU email verification, driver ratings, and sharing my trip live with a friend makes me feel safe.” — Surveyed Texas A&M Student Passenger (Skipped grocery runs several times due to lack of ride)
The Solution

Meet Aggie Ride.

A student-powered way to turn individual travel plans into shared journeys. It’s not about hailing a driver on demand — it’s about discovering who is already heading in your direction.

01
explore

Share your intent

Tell the community where you need to go, your flexible time window, and your starting hub. Or post that you’re driving and have open seats.

02
hub

Discover your match

Aggie Ride groups students with compatible travel intentions into live Destination Clusters — distinguishing between active riders and registered drivers.

03
handshake

Connect & coordinate

Coordinate pickup at 7 predefined campus hubs (Park West, MSC, Century Square), message privately, and board safely with secure OTP passcodes.
